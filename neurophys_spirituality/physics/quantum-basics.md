# Quantum Basics

*Physics concept page — part of the [Physics](index.md) series.*

## The short version

Quantum mechanics describes how matter and energy behave at very small scales. Its most commonly misused ideas — superposition, measurement, and entanglement — are precise, mathematically defined, and none of them involve human consciousness doing anything special.

## Superposition

Before measurement, a quantum system (like an electron) doesn't have a single definite value for certain properties — instead, it's described by a combination ("superposition") of possible values, each with a probability attached. This is not "it could be anything, so anything is possible" — the probabilities are exact, calculable numbers, and they constrain outcomes very precisely. Superposition has been demonstrated in tightly controlled lab experiments with photons, electrons, and even large molecules.

## Measurement / "collapse"

When a quantum system is measured, it's found to have one specific value, with a likelihood set by the superposition. What counts as a "measurement" in this technical sense is an interaction with another physical system — a detector, a photon, an atom — that becomes correlated with the quantum system in question and effectively records the outcome. It is not "observation by a conscious being." A photon detector measures a photon whether or not any person ever looks at the readout. This is arguably the single most consequential public misunderstanding of quantum mechanics, because it's the seed of most "consciousness creates reality" claims.

## Entanglement

Two particles can be prepared so that measuring one instantly tells you something about the other, no matter how far apart they are. This is real and experimentally confirmed (and won the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics). But entanglement cannot be used to send information faster than light, and it doesn't imply any kind of causal or mental "connection" in the everyday sense — the correlation is set at the moment the particles are prepared, not created by anyone's attention.

## What gets borrowed (and misused)

- **"The observer effect proves consciousness creates reality"** — conflates a technical physics term (measurement/interaction with an instrument) with the folk meaning of "observe" (a person looking at something). The equations of quantum mechanics don't reference minds, intentions, or awareness anywhere.
- **"Quantum" as a synonym for "magical" or "unlimited potential"** — used to imply that because quantum systems are probabilistic, anything is possible if you just believe it enough. Quantum probabilities are fixed, calculable, and don't respond to belief or intention.
- **"We're all entangled, so we're all connected"** — real entanglement requires a specific physical preparation process between particles; it isn't a background state of the universe that links people's minds.

See the [claims index](../claims/index.md) for specific claims (quantum consciousness, manifestation, Dispenza-style "quantum healing") addressed in more detail.

## What's still genuinely open

The interpretation of quantum mechanics — *what's actually happening* during measurement, and whether collapse is physically real or just an update in our knowledge — is a live, serious debate among physicists (Copenhagen, Many-Worlds, pilot-wave, objective collapse theories, etc.). This is a genuine frontier. None of the mainstream interpretations require or even suggest that consciousness plays a special causal role, though a small minority of physicists (and a much larger number of non-physicists) have argued otherwise — worth its own dedicated page.
